The Gut-Spine Connection

Digestion Starts With Your Nervous System

Your gut and brain are in constant communication through a bidirectional pathway called the gut-brain axis. Your gut contains over 100 million neurons — often called your "second brain" — and hosts over 100 trillion microorganisms that produce neurotransmitters including over 90% of your body's serotonin. The spine houses the central nervous system, which serves as the master relay for gut-brain signaling.

When your spine is properly aligned, signals flow clearly between your brain and digestive organs. When spinal misalignments compress or irritate the nerves that supply your digestive system, communication breaks down — and so does your digestion.

The Vagus Nerve Connection

The vagus nerve is the longest cranial nerve in your body and the key link between your brain and your gut. Here's why it matters for your digestive health.

The vagus nerve is your primary gut-brain communication line
It originates in the brainstem near the upper cervical vertebrae (C1-C2)
80-90% of vagus nerve fibers carry signals from gut to brain
Upper cervical misalignment can compress and irritate the vagus nerve
Chiropractic adjustments restore vagal tone and nerve flow
Improved vagal function supports digestion, immune response, and mood
