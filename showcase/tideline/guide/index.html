Concept & process

Tideline demonstrates restraint. Instead of density or spectacle, it uses rhythm, white space, low contrast, and a literary voice. The architecture follows a magazine sequence: cover, prologue, essay, visual study, closing quotation.

Technique & implementation

The original moon-ocean landscape was generated for this composition. Newsreader supplies the soft editorial voice; fluid type scales preserve proportion. Scroll parallax holds the hero in suspension, while the full-screen horizon can be dragged vertically or adjusted with arrow keys.

Three passes

01 / Composition & art direction

Found the quiet hierarchy

  • Aligned the headline to the image’s staircase and wave rather than centering it mechanically.
  • Added a coral editorial rule to the prologue and a ghosted issue folio behind the close.
  • Reduced saturation and contrast so typography and atmosphere share one tonal system.
02 / Motion & interaction

Slowed the digital pace

  • Introduced subtle hero drift and long, blurred section dissolves.
  • Built the draggable sky/sea horizon with bounded pointer tracking.
  • Added arrow-key parity and clear focus treatment for the experimental control.
03 / Polish & technical quality

Preserved calm on mobile

  • Reflowed editorial grids, removed nonessential marginalia, and protected line length.
  • Eliminated overflow and made navigation targets easier to reach.
  • Removed blur, parallax, and float motion when reduced motion is requested.

Tools

Codex image generation, HTML, CSS Grid, fluid clamp typography, Pointer Events, IntersectionObserver, and a small vanilla JavaScript controller.
