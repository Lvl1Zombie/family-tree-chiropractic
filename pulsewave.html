What Is PulseWave Therapy

The Science of Sound Wave Healing

PulseWave therapy — also known as extracorporeal shockwave therapy (ESWT) — is a non-invasive treatment that delivers high-energy acoustic waves into damaged soft tissue, tendons, and bones. These rapid acoustic pulses penetrate deep into the treatment area, triggering increased blood flow, release of growth factors, breakdown of calcifications and scar tissue, and formation of new blood vessels. Your body treats the acoustic energy as a healing signal, activating its own repair mechanisms at the cellular level.

How It Works

Your Treatment, Step by Step

Each PulseWave session follows a precise protocol designed to maximize acoustic energy delivery and accelerate your body's healing response.

Conductive gel is applied to the skin over the treatment area
A handheld applicator delivers rapid acoustic wave pulses into tissue
Acoustic energy breaks down scar tissue and calcifications
Blood flow increases, delivering growth factors and nutrients
New blood vessels form in the treatment area (neovascularization)
Cellular repair and tissue regeneration accelerate
What We Treat

From Chronic Pain
to Full Recovery.

PulseWave therapy is effective for a wide range of musculoskeletal conditions, including plantar fasciitis, Achilles tendinopathy, tennis elbow, golfer's elbow, rotator cuff injuries, shoulder tendinopathy, bursitis, shin splints, heel spurs, hip pain, and chronic tendinopathy.

Treatment sessions last approximately 5 minutes. Most patients need 3 to 6 sessions spaced 3 to 10 days apart, and many notice significant relief after just 2 to 3 sessions. PulseWave is often combined with chiropractic adjustments for comprehensive results.
